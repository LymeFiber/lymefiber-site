LymeFiber’s connection to you has three main components: 

- the fiber cable that runs on public utility poles (known as the “fiber pass”)
- the “drop” line that connects your home or business to the fiber pass, and
- the NID (network interface device) on the outside, and the "ONT" or router inside the building

**Aerial drop:** A standard aerial drop runs from the nearest public utility pole to your building.
LymeFiber installs this for the standard installation fee, as long as the drop distance is no more than 400 feet.
If you have an aerial drop that exceeds 400 feet or the
drop is underground, you have a "non-standard drop".

**Non-standard drop:** If you think you have a non-standard drop, please review the options below.
Payment for non-standard dropscan be added to your
monthly bill in 3 to 12 month increments
(interest free), depending on the total cost
(see table below).
Make a request for installment payments in advance
by sending a request to [Billing@LymeFiber.net](mailto:billing@LymeFiber.net).

## Underground connections through existing conduit

- If you have usable conduit (clear of breaks or debris and with functioning pull string) in place and are within 400 feet of the nearest utility pole, we can complete the exterior connection at no additional cost. Longer connections in existing conduit are treated the same as aerial drops.
  Drops over 400 feet incur a charge of $1 per foot for additional footage aerial or underground.
- If you plan to install conduit with a pull string
  by hiring your own contractor please contact us
  to let us know that you intend to do so.

**NOTE:** It may be possible to get a pull string through your existing conduit by yourself or by hiring a contractor (usually an electrician.). If you do so:

-   Shorter, newer, and wider conduits are the best for this.
-   Some people have used a shop vac and/or leaf blower. [Here are some guidelines](https://www.doityourself.com/forum/communications-voice-radio-data-communications/427655-using-shop-vac-pull-string-thru-conduit.html).

In shorter underground drops, rigid snakes can sometimes
be used to install a pull string.

## When conduit is required

-   LymeFiber follows the existing utilities to a structure.
If those utilities are underground, the drop fiber
must be installed in conduit
from the utility pole to the building.
- The National Safety Electrical Code provides that existing utilities set the precedent for installations of additional utilities, and LymeFiber’s policy is to follow that practice. We will not install fiber aerially if we are the only utility doing so.
Without other utilities "above", the drop fiber is not strong enough to survive brances falling on it.
Experience shows that when we have not followed the
policy, damage from storms and other issues resulted
in frequent repair and replacement, and that those customers eventually installed conduit instead.
-   Fiber cannot be shared in a conduit that has power in it.
-   Fiber can be shared in conduit with phone, _provided:_ 
  1) the conduit is big enough to accommodate the addition of fiber (a 2 inch conduit can usually accommodate 2 lines); 
  2) a pull string is present that meets our specifications (below); and 
  3) there are no pedestals along the conduit path owned by other utilities.
-   LymeFiber cannot and will not touch other utility's cables. We will not use an existing, even abandoned, cable to pull our fiber through an existing conduit. If a homeowner wishes to abandon an existing cable to make room for our fiber, they can use that cable to pull their own string through.
  We can then use that string to pull our cable through the conduit.

## Conduit specifications

-   _Whoever is doing the excavation work must first Premark the area and then call DIG SAFE! (811) It is the law._
- Conduit should go from the utility pole nearest to your house and follow the route of other underground utilities.
- Conduit must be a minimum of 1.5 inch diameter for distances under 300 feet.
  For a run longer than 300 feet, 2 inch diameter conduit should be installed. Conduit must be “schedule 40 electrical” (not water pipe), and use long sweeps (not 90-degree elbows or “L” shaped angles)
    - Fiber cannot be bent at sharp angles as it will degrade the signal and will ultimately break.
    - If you have an extremely long run, please call our office for further instructions.
- Conduit over 400 feet require intermediate pull boxes or pedestals. Please call our office to discuss your specific circumstance before construction commences so we can ensure proper design and installation.
- Conduit must come up at least 1.5 to 2 feet above grade at both the house and pole and be secured both to the pole and to the house or backboard. Also, conduit on the pole side must come up in the same quadrant (same side of the pole) as existing services. Conduit on the house side **_MUST_** come up on the outside of the house.
-   Conduit should be trenched 18-24 inches deep.
-   Customers _**MUST**_ install a tracer wire (14-16 gauge coated on top of the conduit for Dig Safe and future location, as LymeFiber does **NOT** use “toneable” cable.
-   Pull strings should have a 210-250lb tensile-strength.
-   The pull string should extend at least 2 feet beyond the ends of the conduit and be tied around the outside of the conduit with an unglued cap over the ends of the conduit to prevent water from entering.
After fiber is installed, we use a specialized putty to plug the conduit.
-   No weather heads please. There is too high a risk for fiber to break and interrupt service.
